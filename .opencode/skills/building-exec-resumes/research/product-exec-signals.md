# What hiring CEOs, VCs, and recruiters screen for in product executives

*Research gathered 2026-09-29 by Claude subagents for the resume framework. Evidence tiers are marked inline. Verify anything important before quoting it publicly.*

Research date: 2026-09-29. Scope: VP Product / CPO / Head of Product at B2B technical companies under ~2,000 people. Web research only.

## Confidence key

- **[E] Evidence**: survey data or a firm's published research. Still check the sample.
- **[X] Named expert**: named practitioner, investor, or search partner writing under their own name.
- **[G] Generic / marketing**: resume-writer, staffing-firm, or SEO content. Low confidence; used only where it agrees with [X] sources.

## Source-quality warnings

- **Products That Count CPO reports** ([2025](https://productsthatcount.com/2025-cpo-insights-report/), [2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/2026-cpo-insights-report-predicts-product-managers-will-disappear-by-2030-302782732.html)) come from a nonprofit community that also runs CPO awards. They publish no methodology, and the full reports sit behind a membership login. The P&L figure conflicts across sources: one search snippet said "70% of CPOs manage a P&L", but I could not verify it. The 2026 press release says "over half." Cite "over half (self-reported, PTC 2026)" at most.
- **"Most CEO-CPO relationships fail within 18 months"** ([Keith Cowing, 2026-04-07](https://keithcowing.com/content/hiring-first-head-of-product-ceo-mistakes)) has no source. Don't repeat it as fact.
- **"68% of Senior PM to VP promotions fail within 18 months"** ([Aakash Gupta, 2026-04-10](https://www.aakashg.com/vp-of-product/)) also has no source. Don't use it.
- **Lauren Ipsen episode on Lenny's podcast** ([2022-11-03](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/lessons-from-one-of-the-worlds-top)): the transcript is paywalled. One automated summary claimed she talked about GM experience. A verbatim check of the public page found no mention of GM or P&L, so that claim was removed. Only the chapter topics can be verified: "three main types of PMs", "staying long enough to have a meaningful impact", and "resume red flags and the importance of honesty".
- Lenny Rachitsky has no public article on hiring VPs or CPOs. His relevant material is either paywalled or covers PM hiring in general.

## Q1. What CEOs and founders say they screen for in a VP Product or CPO

### Named-expert consensus (strong agreement across sources)

1. **Developing the team comes first.** Marty Cagan (SVPG) names four competencies: team development, product vision, execution, and product culture. His line: "The single most important responsibility of any VP Product is to develop a strong team." He wants "proven ability to develop others", which means a track record of recruiting and coaching people. [SVPG, The VP Product Role, undated](https://www.svpg.com/the-vp-product-role/); [Product Managers vs Product Leaders, 2023-12-12](https://www.svpg.com/product-managers-vs-product-leaders/). [X]
2. **The leader should complement the CEO.** Cagan: "The VP Product needs to complement the CEO." A visionary CEO needs an executor, and a CEO without a product vision needs a visionary. Elad Gil agrees and warns against hiring only a "process person". [Elad Gil, High Growth Handbook, 2018](https://growth.eladgil.com/book/chapter-7-product-management/hiring-a-strong-vp-product/). [X]
3. **They must have owned a product.** Jason Lemkin: hire "a true head of product," someone who "has owned a product" before, not a project manager. [SaaStr/LinkedIn, 2018-06-01](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/when-should-you-hire-vp-product-jason-m-lemkin). [X]
4. **They must have actually driven the roadmap.** a16z's talent team (Caroline Horn, Charles Hubbard, Jenna Zucker) favors candidates who drove roadmaps over "supporting players at larger companies." They also want a revenue mindset: pricing, packaging, bundling. [a16z, Hiring a Chief Product Officer, 2023-06-14](https://a16z.com/hiring-a-chief-product-officer/). [X]
5. **Commercial and customer depth, not only craft.** Gibson Biddle's head-of-product rubric covers seven competencies: technical, management/communication, creativity, business acumen (models, pricing, shareholder value), marketing, design, and consumer science. It also includes a ladder: build a product, build a successful product, build an organization, build a company, build an industry. [Biddle, 2017-10-13](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-your-next-head-product-looks-like-gibson-biddle). [X]
6. **Outcomes, reputation, and tenure.** Aakash Gupta and Colin Lernell list three things hiring CEOs evaluate: reputation (including backchannel references), multi-year outcomes, and domain or stage-matched experience. They quote Andrew Abramson (Riviera): list outcomes like incremental revenue, not features shipped. They also treat short stints as a negative. [Product Leadership Job Search, 2024-04-18](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/product-leadership-job-search). [X]
7. **Solving the right problem matters more than prestige.** Keith Cowing's panel of recruiters (Chris Running, Jodi Jefferson, Michael Cullen) and CEO Dennis Crowley describes the "logo trap." CEOs often hire the person they think they should want instead of the person they need for the next 12 to 18 months. The panel also says a candidate who reflexively claims "I don't micromanage" is a red flag, because staying close to the details matters at early stages. [Cowing, 2026-04-07](https://keithcowing.com/content/hiring-first-head-of-product-ceo-mistakes). [X]

### Evidence

- **Russell Reynolds CPO study:** "previous titles or functions are less important" than breadth. That breadth spans customer-centric, strategic, technical-literacy, and commercial-acumen competencies (including pricing). The study says span across all four is rare, so companies make trade-offs. [RRA, 2020-12-18](https://www.russellreynolds.com/en/insights/reports-surveys/the-customer-first-organization-the-rise-of-the-chief-product-officer--full-2021-edition). [E]

### Implication for the resume

The first page should prove four things: (a) you owned product outcomes, including commercial ones; (b) you built and developed a team, with named promotions and hires; (c) you partnered with the CEO on strategy; and (d) you stayed long enough to see results.

## Q2. Stage differences: Series B–D, later-stage or public, and big-tech VP

### Named expert

- **Hire for the next 12 to 18 months.** Elad Gil: a shorter horizon under-hires and a longer one over-hires. [Gil, Hiring executives](https://growth.eladgil.com/book/chapter-4-building-the-executive-team/hiring-executives/). [X]
- **Big-company executives bring two mismatches.** Ben Horowitz names a *rhythm mismatch* (waiting for interrupts) and a *skill-set mismatch*: running an org means decision-making, prioritization, and org design, while building one means hiring and creating process from scratch. His red flags: first-month plans that over-emphasize learning, and equity as the main motivation. His green flag is a candidate proposing more initiatives than seem feasible. [a16z, 2010-04-21](https://a16z.com/why-is-it-hard-to-bring-big-company-execs-into-little-companies/). [X]
- **Leader archetypes** from Shreyas Doshi: Operator (scales teams, alignment, execution), Craftsperson (strategy and product insight, weaker with large-org overhead), and Visionary. Operators tend to be needed after product-market fit, as the company scales. [Doshi thread, Mar 2021](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1375491623308550144); [20VC episode](https://www.thetwentyminutevc.com/shreyas-doshi). [X]
- **Kinds of product work** from Casey Winters (ex-CPO Eventbrite): tech and process scaling, new product-market fit, iterating features on existing fit, and growth. Leaders fail through vision misalignment with the founder, an expertise-to-business-model mismatch, or failure to adapt. [Why Product Leaders Fail, 2021-05-18](https://www.caseyaccidental.com/p/why-product-leaders-fail). [X]
- **RRA archetypes**: Innovators (startups), Optimizers (heads of function), Scalers (technology-deep, mature orgs). RRA describes the trajectory "from being builders to architects, to commercial leaders" and says companies increasingly expect CPOs to act as "Digital GMs." [RRA, 2020](https://www.russellreynolds.com/en/insights/reports-surveys/the-customer-first-organization-the-rise-of-the-chief-product-officer--full-2021-edition). [E/X]
- **Early stage wants hands-on leaders.** Riviera's Andrew Abramson: Series A companies usually want a hands-on PM who could grow into a VP. A true VP search makes sense after Series A, with 12 to 15+ engineers and a VP Engineering in place. [Riviera, 2019-08-27](https://www.rivierapartners.com/insights/questions-to-answer-before-hiring-a-vp-product-and-mistakes-to-avoid/). [X]
- **Cowing's panel, "altitude vs trajectory":** don't chase the expensive "cruising altitude" candidate over someone on a rising path. [2026](https://keithcowing.com/content/hiring-first-head-of-product-ceo-mistakes). [X]
- **Staffing-firm view (lower confidence):** KORE1 (Tom Kenaley) says a Series A founder and a public-company CEO writing "VP of Product" are describing "two different humans." It sketches the stages: pre-PMF head of product still writes specs; Series B–C VP manages PMs and squads; growth or pre-IPO VP runs managers of managers. [KORE1, 2026-07-31](https://www.kore1.com/how-to-hire-vp-product-2026/). [G]

### What to emphasize by target

| Target | Emphasize | De-emphasize |
|---|---|---|
| Series B–D (under 500 people) | Hands-on product judgment; building an org from scratch (first PM/design hires, rituals); speed; GTM partnership; pricing and packaging; founder partnership | Process at scale, governance, "navigated a matrixed org" |
| Later-stage (500 to 2,000 people, pre-IPO or public) | Managers of managers; portfolio strategy; multi-product; planning cadence; board and exec communication; P&L or revenue ownership; M&A | Individual-contributor-style shipping details |
| Big tech VP (e.g., GitHub/Microsoft) | Scale metrics; cross-org influence; platform/ecosystem; operating in a matrix | Scrappiness; building from zero |

For someone moving from a large company into smaller ones, Horowitz's and a16z's warnings are the ones to answer on the page. Show personally driven initiatives with clear attribution ("I set X, which led to Y"), not "supported" or "contributed." GM roles and head-of-product roles at startups are the evidence of a builder rhythm.

## Q3. Showing technical and platform credibility without writing an engineering resume

### Named expert

- Cagan: a VP Product needs a "strong technology background" *plus* business economics. [SVPG](https://www.svpg.com/the-vp-product-role/). [X]
- a16z: for technically sophisticated products, get references from individual engineers, not just engineering leaders. Their interview exercise has the candidate co-build 3-month, 1-year, and 3–5-year priorities with the head of engineering. [a16z, 2023](https://a16z.com/hiring-a-chief-product-officer/). [X]
- Winters: specialization in "tech and process scaling" is a distinct kind of product work. Name it as a strength rather than hiding it. [2021](https://www.caseyaccidental.com/p/why-product-leaders-fail). [X]

### Generic consensus (lower confidence, but consistent)

- Resume-site guidance pairs technical depth (architecture, latency, pipelines) with product outcomes (revenue, adoption, developer experience). [Enhancv, 2026](https://enhancv.com/resume-examples/technical-product-manager/). [G]
- KORE1: "Operational credibility to earn engineering's respect is not optional." [2026](https://www.kore1.com/how-to-hire-vp-product-2026/). [G]

### Synthesis (my inference, not sourced)

- Put the technical noun in the *problem* and the business metric in the *result*. Pattern: "[Customer or business problem] in [technical domain] → [decision you made] → [adoption, revenue, retention, or cost outcome]."
- Show the audience and the business: "developer customers", "enterprise log consumers", data volume or scale, and revenue or retention outcomes. Leave out implementation details: languages, frameworks, and which systems you personally configured.
- Make engineering partnership visible: co-owned roadmap with the VP Eng, platform strategy decisions, build/buy calls.
- Show technical fluency through judgment calls: architecture trade-offs you arbitrated, platform-versus-product sequencing, pricing a usage-based infrastructure product.

## Q4. Presenting AI experience credibly in 2026

### Named expert and investor (2025–2026)

- **Insight Partners** (Sara Clark, Andrew Dunn, 2026-07-17) name six traits, led by intellectual curiosity. The signal is leaders who "experiment with AI personally" and share what they learn. Another positive is ambiguity comfort: "I don't know yet, but here's what we're trying." Their red flag is a CPO unwilling to discuss pricing, which they call "operating in the wrong decade." They also report a 10–15% compensation premium for AI-ready leaders. [Insight Partners](https://www.insightpartners.com/ideas/ai-hiring-product-eng-leaders/). [X]
- **Bessemer** (Atlas, undated, 2025–26 context) says product leaders must engage with model quality and iteration directly, and work in day-to-week cycles rather than quarterly roadmaps. It recommends working-session interviews over prepared stories, and warns against "logo bias." One evaluative question from the piece: "Is the AI getting better, and how do we know?" [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/talent-trends-for-the-ai-native-c-suite). [X]
- **Tomer Cohen** (LinkedIn CPO) on Lenny's podcast (2025-12-04): LinkedIn replaced its APM program with "Full Stack Builders". The value is in customizing agents to your own infrastructure, since generic tools fail on enterprise code. [Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-linkedin-is-replacing-pms). [X]
- **Keith Cowing's recruiter panel** lists AI fluency as core to modern product leadership. [2026-04-07](https://keithcowing.com/content/hiring-first-head-of-product-ceo-mistakes). [X]
- **Aakash Gupta** (2026-04-10) sees AI at VP level as a tool for synthesis and decision memos. Judgment remains the job. [aakashg.com](https://www.aakashg.com/vp-of-product/). [X]

### Evidence (weak)

- Products That Count 2026: "three out of four product leaders" use AI for prototyping, and one in three CPOs control AI M&A budgets. Self-reported; see the warning above. [PR Newswire, 2026-05-27](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/2026-cpo-insights-report-predicts-product-managers-will-disappear-by-2030-302782732.html). [E, low]
- Lenny/TrueUp: 688 open AI PM roles, and a shift toward senior hiring. [2025-05-13](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/state-of-the-product-job-market-in). [E]

### Resume noise versus signal

- **Noise:** "AI strategy," "AI-first mindset," tool lists without outcomes, and buzzword-dense bullets. Fortune (2026-08-10) reports hiring leaders are suspicious of "overfit" AI-tailored resumes and lean on interviews that walk through specific project steps. [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/10/resume-perfect-match-ai-hiring-hr-leaders-interview-matters-most/). [X/G]
- **Signal:** shipped AI features with adoption or revenue outcomes; how you measured quality (evals); how you changed your org's operating model with AI (cycle time, team shape); and personal hands-on building, stated concretely with scale and who uses it. Aakash Gupta and Nancy Li: tie the tool to the business outcome. [2026-04-19](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/ai-product-manager-resume). [X]
- **Where "built MCP servers" or side projects fit:** Insight Partners and Bessemer both reward personal experimentation. For an exec resume, it reads best as proof of curiosity and fluency (one line, with users or outcome), not as a headline accomplishment. A resume led by personal coding projects risks the "engineering resume" read and invites the question of who was running the org. No source addresses MCP specifically. This is inference.
- **Christian & Timbers** (executive-search marketing, 2025-06-28) claims some searches put AI integration among the top three criteria and screen for agent strategy and model cost/latency operating experience. [C&T](https://www.christianandtimbers.com/insights/product-leadership-aligns-with-ai-infrastructure-in-2025). [G, low]

## Q5. Showing scope; is GM a strong signal?

### Named expert

- Gibson Biddle: company size and product category for each role, a clear positioning statement, quantified results, and one page per ten years. Quoting Rajesh Nerlikar: "show me what your impact was." [Ask Gib, 2021-03-08](https://askgib.substack.com/p/what-are-your-top-tips-for-a-pm-resumecv). [X]
- Cagan: team development is the top job, so show hires, coaching, and promotions. [SVPG](https://www.svpg.com/the-vp-product-role/). [X]
- Aakash Gupta: at VP level, the narrative matters more than the resume. Reverse-engineer the company's stage-specific problem from the job posting and tailor to it. [2026](https://www.aakashg.com/vp-of-product/). [X]
- Gupta and Lernell: more companies hire product leaders into GM roles that own P&Ls. They also say 10–30% of Director+ roles and 70–100% of C-suite roles are never posted. The source of that range is not named in the article. [2024](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/product-leadership-job-search). [X]
- KORE1: the real proof is a trail of promoted PMs and successor leaders, plus honest discussion of failed products. [2026](https://www.kore1.com/how-to-hire-vp-product-2026/). [G]

### Is GM a strong signal?

- **For:** RRA's "Digital GM" framing [E]; a16z's revenue mindset and pricing/packaging [X]; Biddle's "build a company" rung [X]; Insight's pricing red flag [X]; Gupta and Lernell on the GM trend [X]; PTC's "over half own P&L" [E, low].
- **Qualifier:** RRA says titles matter less than span. Cagan's model centers on team development and product culture, not P&L. **Conflict:** SVPG-style founders will value GM mainly as commercial judgment, not as a replacement for product-org leadership.
- **Net:** GM or P&L experience is a clear differentiator for CPO and B2B roles, especially where pricing and monetization are in scope. Pair it with evidence of building a product org, or it can read as "business person, not product leader."

### Generic consensus on scope metrics

Retained-search guidance says the summary should lead with scope: P&L or revenue, headcount, and signature outcomes. A VP resume without numbers "forces the reader to guess." [Jobscan / TechnCV](https://techncv.com/blog/executive-resume) [G]. Use scope numbers: org size (PMs, designers, managers of managers), revenue or ARR influenced, customers, and stage change (e.g., "from X to Y").

## Q6. Teardowns and examples

**Gap:** I found no credible public teardowns of VP or CPO resumes by named product-exec recruiters. Lenny's relevant material (the Ipsen resume-red-flags segment) is paywalled. The examples on Resume Worded, LiveCareer, Teal, and similar sites are template marketing [G].

Patterns that hold across named sources and the generic material:

1. **A positioning line at the top**, naming who you are and what problem you solve (Biddle, Gupta).
2. **A scope line per role**: company size, stage, product category, team size (Biddle; generic executive guidance).
3. **3–4 outcome bullets for recent roles, 1–2 for older ones** (Biddle).
4. **At least one team-building bullet per leadership role**: hires, promotions, org design (Cagan, KORE1).
5. **At least one commercial bullet**: pricing, packaging, revenue, P&L (a16z, RRA, Insight).
6. **Clear attribution**: "I led / decided / set", not "supported" (a16z, Horowitz).
7. **Honesty and consistency with LinkedIn**: backchannel checks are standard (Ipsen chapter topic, Gupta and Lernell, Fortune).
8. **Tenure**: show multi-year impact per role (Gupta and Lernell; Ipsen chapter topic).

## Unresolved conflicts

- **Resume as filter or business card:** Gupta and Biddle downplay the resume in favor of narrative and network. Retained-search guidance treats the top-of-page summary as the filter. Both hold: the resume gets you past the screen, and the narrative gets you hired.
- **Technical background:** Cagan requires a strong technology background, while RRA says titles and functions matter less than span. For devtools and infra targets, the technical side weighs more (a16z engineer references; KORE1).
- **AI hands-on:** investors reward personal experimentation (Insight, Bessemer, Cohen), and Gupta frames AI at VP level as a judgment aid. Show both, weighted toward org and product outcomes.
