# Executive (VP+) resume norms: what the evidence says

*Research gathered 2026-09-29 by Claude subagents for the resume framework. Evidence tiers are marked inline. Verify anything important before quoting it publicly.*

*Research date: 2026-09-29. Scope: VP Product / SVP / CPO roles at B2B infra, devtools, data, security, and AI-infra companies under 2,000 people.*

Few strong sources exist for VP+ resumes specifically. The big retained firms (Spencer Stuart, Heidrick, Korn Ferry, Russell Reynolds, Egon Zehnder) publish almost nothing on resume format. What they do publish covers process, references, and verification. Most "executive resume" content online comes from resume-writing vendors. Recruiter-attributed advice worth using comes from Korn Ferry CEO Gary Burnison's columns, Spencer Stuart's candidate guide, one HBR piece, one Bespoke Partners piece, and a 2024 newsletter that interviewed named boutique tech-search recruiters from Riviera, Daversa, and True Search.

## Evidence tiers used below

- **Evidence:** a study with a stated methodology (sample, design), or a primary statement of process from the firm doing the hiring or search.
- **Named practitioner:** a named recruiter or search-firm leader, in their own publication or quoted directly. This is opinion, but opinion from informed people.
- **Consensus opinion:** repeated across many sources with no underlying study behind it.
- **Vendor claim:** resume-writing, ATS-optimization, or job-board companies with a commercial stake. Lower confidence.

## 1. How executive recruiters and hiring CEOs read VP+ resumes

### The "6 to 7 seconds" stat: where it comes from and its limits

- **Original study (2012), TheLadders.** Thirty "professional recruiters" were eye-tracked over 10 weeks. The study reported "recruiters spend only 6 seconds reviewing an individual resume" for the initial "fit/no fit" decision. About 80% of that time went to six data points: name, current title and company, previous title and company, start and end dates for both, and education. The author was Will Evans, Head of UX at TheLadders. The report ends by promoting TheLadders' own resume-rewriting service and says TheLadders profiles were "55% easier to read than LinkedIn". [Report PDF, hosted by BU](https://www.bu.edu/com/files/2018/10/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf). **Vendor study with a clear conflict of interest.** It was never peer-reviewed, and it doesn't say which job levels the recruiters were screening for.
- **2018 update, TheLadders.** Reported 7.4 seconds. What worked: simple layouts, clear section headings, bold titles, bulleted accomplishments. What hurt: multiple columns, clutter, long sentences, keyword stuffing. It recommended two pages maximum. [PR Newswire, 2018-11-06](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ladders-updates-popular-recruiter-eye-tracking-study-with-new-key-insights-on-how-job-seekers-can-improve-their-resumes-300744217.html); [HR Dive, 2018-11-08](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/). The public materials don't disclose sample size or recruiter types.
- **Critique.** Recruiter Susan Gygax points out the study doesn't specify positions, resume lengths, or recruiter experience. She also asks whether recruiters knew they were being timed, and argues that executive and retail screening shouldn't share one number. [Spectacle Talent Partners](https://spectacletalentpartners.com/is-the-6-second-resume-scan-a-myth/). (Undated. Practitioner opinion.)
- **How to use it.** Treat "6 to 7 seconds" as the length of the *first-pass triage*, not the full read. The robust finding is *what* gets looked at first: titles, companies, dates, progression. That lines up with the named-practitioner advice below.

### Named practitioners on what they scan for

- **Spencer Stuart (June 2023).** "Your resume is not just a document designed to simply list titles and metrics; it is a chance to tell your leadership story." They also say "The majority of our candidates are not actively seeking a new job", so retained search starts from sourcing, not from a resume pile. Spencer Stuart requires your latest resume before it presents you to a client. [Spencer Stuart, "Your Experience as a Candidate"](https://www.spencerstuart.com/research-and-insight/your-experience-as-a-candidate). **Primary process statement.**
- **Gary Burnison, Korn Ferry CEO (2026-07-20).** "It's all about accomplishment, not just activity." Recruiters look for "clear progression—increasingly larger roles, broader responsibilities and greater impact." He also warns against exaggerated metrics, and says that for every hour spent on a resume you should spend ten networking. [Korn Ferry, "A Résumé Redux"](https://www.kornferry.com/insights/special-edition/a-resume-redux).
- **Burnison (2019-07-03)** on "the best resume in 20 years". It was easy to read, told a progression story with no gaps, gave quantified accomplishments, was truthful with verifiable LinkedIn and portfolio links, avoided clichés, and arrived through a recommendation. "Most unsolicited resumes go unopened." [Korn Ferry](https://www.kornferry.com/insights/this-week-in-leadership/best-resume-in-20-years).
- **Boutique tech search, product leaders specifically (2024-04-18).** Aakash Gupta and Colin Lernell interviewed executive recruiters, including Theresa Skelly (Riviera Partners), Matt Johnson (Paradigm, ex-Daversa), Andrew Abramson (Fusion Talent, ex-Riviera and ex-Korn Ferry), Alex Klein (Nucleus, ex-True Search), Bobby Gormsen, and Vidur Dewan. They reported three screening criteria: **reputation** (past companies, managers, third-party validation), **outcomes and impact**, and **domain expertise**. Supporting signals: promotions roughly every 12 to 18 months, tenure of 3+ years, and no back-to-back one-year stints. Quotes:
  - Abramson: "Don't just list accomplishments as shipping a certain number of features. This signals a feature factory mentality. Instead, focus on the outcomes and impact, like incremental revenue or new channels."
  - Johnson: "some clients wouldn't even have a first conversation unless we had at least 1-2 backdoor references from people they trust or know."
  - Skelly (Riviera): clients often weigh "super specific domain experience" against "a general athlete with best in class experience from a tangential high growth company."
  - [Aakash Gupta, "The Product Leadership Job Search"](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/product-leadership-job-search). **Named practitioners. The most relevant source found for B2B tech product leadership roles.**
- **Chelsea Mansfield, Bespoke Partners (SaaS/PE software exec search, 2025-06-04).** "A pretty resume won't cut it." She wants claims that are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant to value-creation metrics (churn, ARR, NRR, CAC), and Time-bound. She rejects vague phrases like "leading initiatives" and "driving growth", and says "Pedigree matters far less than performance in private equity." [Bespoke Partners](https://www.bespokepartners.com/a-pretty-resume-wont-cut-it-using-smart-criteria-to-showcase-true-value-creation/).
- **Lindsay Mustain, ex-Amazon recruiter (CNBC, 2024-02-08).** Duty-style bullets ("Miss America answers") appear "all the way up to the C-suite." "Your eyes go straight to the numbers." [CNBC via NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/the-no-1-resume-mistake-according-to-ex-amazon-recruiter-you-see-it-all-the-way-up-to-the-c-suite/5118082/). This comes from high-volume corporate recruiting, not exec search.

### What makes them pass (synthesis)

In decreasing order of how well it's supported:

1. No warm path or references. Burnison, Johnson, and Thompson/Loflin (see §6) all make this point.
2. Titles and companies that don't read as the right level or domain.
3. Duties instead of outcomes.
4. A pattern of short tenures with no explanation.
5. Hard-to-scan layout.

The resume matters less than the network at this level, but it still gets read and verified.

## 2. Length, how far back, and age bias

### Length

| Source | Guidance | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| [Korn Ferry, 2025-01-07](https://www.kornferry.com/insights/this-week-in-leadership/5-resume-resolutions-for-2025) | "Résumés shouldn't be pages-long treatises"; most systems accept two pages; prioritize the last decade | Named firm |
| [Ladders 2018](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ladders-updates-popular-recruiter-eye-tracking-study-with-new-key-insights-on-how-job-seekers-can-improve-their-resumes-300744217.html) | Two pages max | Vendor study |
| [ResumeGo 2018](https://www.resumego.net/research/one-or-two-page-resumes/) | 482 recruiters, hiring managers, HR, and some C-suite screened 7,712 resumes in a simulation. Two-page resumes were chosen 2.9× more often for managerial roles. Reviewers spent 4:05 on two-page resumes vs 2:24 on one-page. | Vendor study. **Confounded:** the two-page versions had 700–850 words vs 350–500, so it measures more content, not length. Also a simulation, not real hiring. |
| [Meg Guiseppi, 2025-02-17](https://executivecareerbrand.com/c-level-executive-resume-length-one-two-or-three-pages/) | 2–3 pages OK if the content warrants it; page one must stand alone | Vendor (exec resume writer) |
| [Ask a Manager (Alison Green)](https://www.askamanager.org/2020/02/my-step-by-step-guide-to-writing-a-resume.html) | Two pages is fine for anyone past a few years' experience | Consensus opinion |

**Conflict:** 2 pages vs 3 pages. Better-supported side: **2 pages**. Everyone not selling resume-writing says 2 or fewer. The 3-page allowance comes mostly from vendors, and none of the evidence supports going past 2. One widely repeated stat ("Ladders 2024 survey: 81% of executive recruiters prefer two pages, 12% accept three") appears only on a resume-vendor blog ([Resume Optimizer Pro](https://resumeoptimizerpro.com/blog/best-resume-formats-for-senior-executives)), and I could not find the original. **Treat it as unverified. Don't cite it.**

### How far back

- Korn Ferry says to prioritize the most recent decade ([2025](https://www.kornferry.com/insights/this-week-in-leadership/5-resume-resolutions-for-2025)). Burnison's 2020 advice for experienced candidates is to give about 75% of the experience detail to the most recent role ([Korn Ferry, 2020-02-20](https://www.kornferry.com/insights/this-week-in-leadership/three-best-resumes)).
- **Consensus opinion (vendors):** detail the last 10–15 years. Compress earlier roles into a single "Early career" or "Prior experience" line, often without dates. [TopResume](https://topresume.com/career-advice/10-powerful-changes-for-your-senior-level-resume); [Briefcase Coach](https://www.briefcasecoach.com/how-far-resume/).
- **Tension with the progression signal.** Burnison and the tech-search recruiters value visible progression. Cutting too much hides a 21-year arc from designer/IC to Sr. Director. Compressing earlier roles keeps the arc visible while dropping the detail.

### Age bias (candidate is 48)

- **Evidence: field experiment.** Neumark, Burn & Button (Journal of Political Economy, 2019) sent 40,000+ applications to 13,000+ jobs in 12 cities. Ages were 29–31, 49–51, and 64–66, signaled through graduation year. They found robust age discrimination against older women, especially near retirement, and much weaker evidence for men. [NBER w21669](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21669); design details in [BLS Monthly Labor Review summary](https://blsmon1.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2017/beyond-bls/is-there-age-discrimination-in-hiring.htm). **Limit:** the jobs tested were admin, retail, janitor, and security guard, not executive roles. It shows that **graduation year works as an age signal** for screeners, but it doesn't measure the size of the effect at VP level.
- **Evidence: survey.** AARP surveyed workers 50+ (n=901 in 2024, n=1,656 in 2025). 64% reported seeing or experiencing age discrimination, 37% of that in job searches. The most common stereotypes were being less tech-savvy (33%) and resistant to change (24%). [AARP 2025](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/work-finances-retirement/employers-workforce/age-discrimination-workplace/). These are self-reports.
- **Live legal signal.** In *Mobley v. Workday*, Judge Rita Lin conditionally certified a nationwide ADEA collective for applicants 40+ screened by Workday's AI tools since Sept 2020 (2025-05-16). [Holland & Knight, 2025-05](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/federal-court-allows-collective-action-lawsuit-over-alleged); [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/employment-labor-and-benefits/2025/05/ai-hiring-age-discrimination-federal-court-workday). These are allegations, not findings of fact.
- **Practical implication (consensus opinion, consistent with the evidence):**
  - Remove graduation years.
  - Don't list 20+ years in full detail.
  - Counter the two measured stereotypes directly: tech-savviness (current AI and infra work) and resistance to change (recent transformations, new operating models).
  - Keep LinkedIn trimmed the same way so the two documents don't contradict each other (see §6).

## 3. Structure

### Summary/profile vs objective
- **Objective statements are out** (consensus). Alison Green: an objective "adds nothing and takes up space" and makes a resume look dated or entry-level. [Ask a Manager](https://www.askamanager.org/2020/02/my-step-by-step-guide-to-writing-a-resume.html).
- **A short professional summary is recommended** for experienced candidates (Burnison, [2020](https://www.kornferry.com/insights/this-week-in-leadership/three-best-resumes)). Spencer Stuart's "leadership story" framing supports a top-of-page narrative.
- Vendor claims about exact word counts (e.g., "120–160 words, 3 bullets") have no evidence behind them.

### "Core competencies" keyword blocks
- **Mixed.** Burnison (2020) suggested keywords like "strategic planning" and "cost management" in the summary. Korn Ferry (2025) and Burnison (2026) say to use the job description's keywords. Ladders 2018 penalized **keyword stuffing**.
- The case for a block is mostly an ATS argument (vendor). Vendor sources themselves now suggest putting competencies inside bullets and the summary rather than in a standalone block ([Resume Worded](https://resumeworded.com/core-competencies-on-resume-key-advice)).
- **Better-supported position:** no evidence that a keyword block helps with human or retained-search readers. Use a short, specific, tailored line only if it earns its space. A generic "Strategic Leadership | Cross-functional Collaboration" row reads as filler and runs into the cliché warnings (Burnison 2019).

### Per-role scope lines
- **Named practitioners and consensus:** show scale of teams, budgets, and projects ([Staffing Advisors, 2025-04-16](https://www.staffingadvisors.com/blog/what-recruiters-want-to-see-in-your-linkedin-profile/)).
- Burnison (2020): add a **company description for lesser-known organizations** ([Korn Ferry](https://www.kornferry.com/insights/this-week-in-leadership/three-best-resumes)). This matters most for startups, acquired companies, and smaller earlier employers.
- Recommended pattern (consensus, no study): one italic context line per role covering what the company does and its stage or size, plus team size, reporting line, and product scope. Then 3–5 outcome bullets.

### Board and exec-team exposure
- HBR (Thompson & Loflin, Jan–Feb 2026) says C-suite candidates must win over "board members, the CEO, executive peers" and assessors, and recommends "T-shaped" enterprise thinking. [HBR](https://hbr.org/2026/01/how-to-stand-out-to-c-suite-recruiters); [HBR IdeaCast ep. 1061](https://hbr.org/podcast/2026/01/what-you-need-to-know-about-executive-recruiting). No source gives resume-format advice on this. The inference: name real exec or board-facing work concretely (e.g., "presented X strategy to CEO/exec staff; resulted in Y"). Don't list "board exposure" as a skill.

### Promotions within one company
- Burnison (2026): show "increasingly larger roles, broader responsibilities." The tech-search recruiters value promotion cadence ([Gupta](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/product-leadership-job-search)).
- **Consensus/vendor format:** one company header with titles stacked newest-first, each with its own dates. Recruiters scan by company, so a promotion buried in a paragraph gets missed ([Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/resume-examples-how-to-format-your-promotions/), vendor).

### Short tenures and acquisitions
- **Named practitioners:** 3+ years per role; back-to-back one-year stints are a flag ([Gupta](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/product-leadership-job-search)). Korn Ferry (2022): "Tenure length should not be used as the primary measurement," and context matters ([Korn Ferry](https://www.kornferry.com/insights/featured-topics/interim-talent/job-hoppers-a-red-flag-or-missed-opportunity)).
- **Acquisitions (consensus, no study):** put "(acquired by X, year)" next to the company name, and group pre- and post-acquisition roles when the employment was continuous so it doesn't read as two short stints ([Career Management Inc., 2025-12](https://www.cmicareers.com/2025/12/19/how-to-explain-company-acquisitions-on-your-linkedin-and-resume/); [Dummies](https://www.dummies.com/article/business-careers-money/careers/job-searches/how-to-list-employers-that-have-merged-or-been-acquired-196242/)).
- **Applied:**
  - **A short stint at a startup that was acquired.** "Startup (acquired by Acquirer, 2023)" explains the short stint on its face. If the candidate stayed on at the acquirer, group the two. Otherwise the acquisition note is the explanation.
  - A background check verifies the legal employer, so the listed employer and dates must match what HR records will show (see §6).

## 4. Framing accomplishments at exec level

- **Outcomes over activities** is the most consistent message across every credible source: Burnison 2019, 2020, and 2026; Korn Ferry 2025 ("How did *your* service in that role make the company better, faster, cheaper, more efficient?" — Michaela Buttler, Korn Ferry); Abramson (no feature counts); Mansfield (SMART, value-creation metrics); Mustain. [Korn Ferry 2025](https://www.kornferry.com/insights/this-week-in-leadership/5-resume-resolutions-for-2025).
- **Metrics:** use numbers, dollars, and percentages. "If you can't quantify your results, express why your accomplishment was important" (Sunny Levitt, quoted by [Korn Ferry 2025](https://www.kornferry.com/insights/this-week-in-leadership/5-resume-resolutions-for-2025)).
- **Conflict: precision vs credibility.** Mansfield wants hard numbers like "$4.2M in ARR." Burnison (2026) warns that unrealistic metrics and hyperbole undermine credibility. **Resolution:** both hold. Use precise numbers you can defend in a backchannel reference check, and attribute honestly ("my team's product" vs "I").
- **Scope without P&L (platform/infra PM).** No source addresses this directly. It's a gap in the literature. Inferences from the sources above:
  - Show scale through **team size, number of products, users or customers served, data volume, reliability, and revenue the product *influences or protects***. Label influenced revenue honestly.
  - Show **cost outcomes**: infra cost per unit, support-ticket deflection, time-to-value.
  - Show **decisions**: what you chose *not* to do, and org design.
  - Staffing Advisors' "scale of projects, teams, budgets, or results" is the closest named-firm guidance ([2025](https://www.staffingadvisors.com/blog/what-recruiters-want-to-see-in-your-linkedin-profile/)).
  - Ken Sandy, quoted by Gupta: at Director+ the evaluation shifts from "what I've done and how I did it" to "my reputation" and "real business outcomes."
- **What "strategic" looks like to a CEO.** Thompson & Loflin (HBR 2026) suggest a candidate "vision memo" addressing the company's critical challenges, plus T-shaped enterprise thinking. On a resume that translates into bullets showing a *choice* with *business consequence* (market entry, pricing or packaging change, platform consolidation, build/buy/partner), not "defined strategy for X."

## 5. ATS at exec level: evidence vs myth

- **Retained search mostly bypasses the inbound ATS funnel.** Spencer Stuart says most of its candidates aren't actively looking, so search starts from sourcing ([2023](https://www.spencerstuart.com/research-and-insight/your-experience-as-a-candidate)). Firms still keep your resume in their own database, and Spencer Stuart requires a current one.
- **Company-posted VP roles at sub-2,000 companies usually run through Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, or Workday**, so parsing still matters. Greenhouse accepts doc, docx, pdf, rtf, and txt, and won't parse files over 2.5 MB ([Greenhouse Support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/360052218132-Supported-formats-for-resumes-cover-letters-and-other-candidate-uploads)).
- **The "ATS auto-rejects 75%" claim is a myth.** Enhancv interviewed 25 U.S. recruiters (Sept–Oct 2025). 92% said their ATS doesn't auto-reject on content; 8% configured match-score rejection. All of them use knockout questions (work authorization, location). Specialized roles typically draw fewer than 200 applicants and get deeper vetting. [Enhancv](https://enhancv.com/blog/does-ats-reject-resumes/). **Vendor, n=25, qualitative.**
- **Counter-evidence that filtering is real.** HBS/Accenture "Hidden Workers" (Fuller & Raman, Sept 2021) surveyed 8,720 workers and 2,275 executives in the US, UK, and DE. 88% of employers said their systems screen out qualified candidates, and many use rigid filters such as employment-gap exclusions. [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2021/09/new-study-says-hidden-workers-are-being-excluded/). This is about mid- and high-skill workers generally and is 5 years old.
  - **Reconciling the two:** the ATS rarely "reads and rejects" the resume. **Recruiter-configured filters and knockout questions** do exclude people, and humans stop reviewing once they have a shortlist.
- **Channel matters more than formatting.** Gem analyzed 165M applications and 1.2M hires (June 2021–May 2025). Referrals convert about 11× the inbound rate, and sourced candidates are nearly 8× more likely to be hired than inbound. No seniority breakdown. [Gem 2026 benchmarks](https://www.gem.com/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-2026-recruiting-benchmarks-report). **Vendor, but large dataset.**
- **Recruiter search behavior (vendor).** Jobscan surveyed 384 recruiters: 99.7% use ATS filters; 76% filter by skills and 55% by job title. It also reports that including the target job title correlates with 10.6× more interviews, which is correlational and from Jobscan's own tool data. [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/state-of-the-job-search). Takeaway: say the literal title terms ("VP Product", "Head of Product") and key domain nouns ("observability", "data platform") where they're true.
- **Formatting for 2026.** Mostly consensus, anchored on Ladders 2018:
  - Single column.
  - No text boxes, tables used for layout, or content in headers/footers.
  - Standard section names.
  - A **text-based PDF** for humans and for Greenhouse/Ashby/Lever. Keep a **DOCX** ready: agency and search recruiters often ask for Word so they can reformat or brand it for client submission ([Adecco](https://www.adecco.com/en-us/job-seekers/resources/article/4-reasons-why-recruiters-want-your-resume-in-word-format)), and some sources claim Workday parses DOCX more consistently (vendor, unverified against Workday docs).
  - No evidence on specific fonts. Use a common, legible font at 10–11pt.

## 6. What goes with the resume

- **Warm introduction and references are the actual gate.**
  - Burnison: always get a warm intro; unsolicited resumes mostly go unopened ([2019](https://www.kornferry.com/insights/this-week-in-leadership/best-resume-in-20-years)).
  - Johnson (Paradigm/ex-Daversa): some clients need 1–2 backdoor references before a first conversation.
  - Thompson & Loflin: treat references as a multi-decade project; recruiters will call people beyond your list ([HBR IdeaCast, 2026-01-13](https://hbr.org/podcast/2026/01/what-you-need-to-know-about-executive-recruiting)).
- **LinkedIn.**
  - Retained researchers source from LinkedIn and proprietary databases.
  - Staffing Advisors (exec search): a profile with "just job titles, employers, and dates doesn't offer enough information", and "you could be passed over simply because another candidate had a more complete profile" ([2025](https://www.staffingadvisors.com/blog/what-recruiters-want-to-see-in-your-linkedin-profile/)).
  - Intellerati (search research) notes that the most senior executives are the least likely to keep accurate profiles, so an accurate one is a differentiator ([Intellerati](https://intellerati.com/best-of-blog/linkedin-recruiter-executive-search-research/)).
- **Consistency as a background-check matter.**
  - Spencer Stuart verifies education and runs in-depth background checks on the 2–3 finalists. They say searches have been "held up for months" by checks, and advise getting "ahead of any question marks" ([2023](https://www.spencerstuart.com/research-and-insight/your-experience-as-a-candidate)).
  - HireRight's 2025 Global Benchmark: more than three-quarters of employers found candidate discrepancies in the past year, with employment and education verification the most likely sources ([HireRight, 2025-09](https://www.hireright.com/company/newsroom/identity-fraud-and-candidate-discrepancies-remain-key-concerns-for-employers)).
  - **Evidence-backed rule:** titles, employers, and month/year dates must match HR records. Resume and LinkedIn should agree, and any trimming (e.g., early career) should be the same in both. Burnison's "best resume" included verifiable LinkedIn links.
- **Cover letter.** Survey data conflicts and comes from vendors: ResumeBuilder (948 leaders, 2024) found 26% read cover letters regularly, while other vendor roundups claim ~83% ([Resume Genius](https://resumegenius.com/blog/cover-letter-help/cover-letter-statistics)). No exec-specific evidence. At VP level a short, specific note to the hiring exec or search partner ties in better with the warm-intro evidence than a formal letter does.
- **Vision memo / leadership one-pager.** Thompson & Loflin (HBR 2026) recommend preparing a "bold vision memo" on the company's critical challenges, used during the process rather than as an application attachment. **Named practitioner.**
- **Executive bio.** Vendor sources say search firms build their own candidate dossiers and a third-person bio can feed into them ([Redick Group](https://theredickgroup.com/20-years-of-insights/whats-the-difference-between-executive-rsums-and-executive-bios)). Low confidence. Nice to have.
- **Writing and portfolio.** Burnison's best resume linked a portfolio. For a PM leader with a public writing record, links to 2–3 relevant pieces are consistent with the "verifiable third-party validation" (reputation) criterion the tech-search recruiters describe.

## 7. Common VP+ resume mistakes named by recruiters

1. **Activities instead of outcomes.** Burnison 2026; Mustain ("Miss America answers", "all the way up to the C-suite"); Abramson ("feature factory").
2. **Vague strategic language.** "Leading initiatives" and "driving growth" (Mansfield, Bespoke). Clichés like "team player" (Burnison 2019).
3. **Exaggerated or unrealistic metrics** (Burnison 2026).
4. **Untailored, generic resume.** Korn Ferry 2025 (Val Olson): applying broadly means "you'll be wasting your time". Mass-applying can trigger screening flags (Burnison 2026).
5. **Unexplained short tenures or back-to-back one-year stints** (Gupta's recruiter panel).
6. **Hard-to-scan layout.** Multiple columns, clutter, long sentences, keyword stuffing (Ladders 2018).
7. **Leaning on the resume instead of network and references** (Burnison 2019 and 2026; Johnson; Thompson & Loflin).
8. **Inconsistencies that surface in background checks** (Spencer Stuart; HireRight).
9. **Too much old detail** (Korn Ferry 2025: prioritize the last decade).

## Biggest conflicts and which side has better support

| Question | Side A | Side B | Better supported |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | 2 pages max (Korn Ferry, Ladders) | 2–3 OK (exec resume vendors) | **2 pages.** 3-page support is vendor-only; the "81% prefer 2" stat can't be traced to a source. |
| ATS risk | ATS auto-rejects most resumes (vendor folklore) | Rarely auto-rejects; knockouts and human shortlisting do the work (Enhancv; Gem) | **B**, with HBS's caveat that recruiter-set filters exclude people |
| Keyword/competency block | Helps ATS (vendors, older Burnison) | Filler and a stuffing risk (Ladders 2018; newer vendor advice) | **Tailored keywords in context**, no generic block |
| Hard metrics vs caution | Quantify everything (Mansfield, Korn Ferry) | Exaggeration kills credibility (Burnison 2026) | **Both:** precise, defensible, honestly attributed |
| Tenure | 3+ years per role (boutique tech recruiters) | Tenure isn't the primary measure (Korn Ferry 2022) | Boutique tech recruiters are closer to this market; **explain short stints on the page** (acquisition notes) |
| Cover letter | Mostly read (vendor surveys) | Mostly skipped (ResumeBuilder) | No good evidence either way; the warm-intro note matters more |

## Gaps

- No methodologically sound study of how *retained* search partners read VP+ resumes. Everything is either practitioner opinion or studies of general recruiters.
- No source addresses showing scope without P&L for platform/infra product leaders. The guidance in §4 is inference.
- The public materials for the Ladders 2018 study don't disclose its sample.
